This is something of a workbook, something of a vicarious sermon, and something of a mirror; but not intended to give you the right answer to anything. Jesus and Satan *both* accuse and the spirit convicts us of having **already known all of this**; and yet we must remember it, and practice it, in ways that reorient the heart and mind. That's the meaning of the word "repent", a word often confused with a kind of apology. It's arranged in a set of chapters that are sectioned into "spreads", which are the workbook portion, if you will. Each spread is designed to be read and reflected upon in about 15–20 minutes, with a small practice to try out in your life. You can do one spread a day, or one spread a week, or however you like. The idea is to let the ideas sink in and take root in your life through practice.

This first set of spreads is focused on the ideas of power and leadership. Each spread juxtaposes a common or received teaching in the world of Christianity or greater society against an excerpt of Jesus' words. These are followed by reflective questions and a small practice you can use in your life to actively experience what Jesus felt and meant in his words. The aim is to invite you to take part in meaningful self-experiments that test how gospel-shaped practices bring about the changes we wish to embody. The invitation a church is meant to give.

Below is a map to the three spreads in Chapter 01 and how to use them together over a week:

::: aside
Days 1–3: One spread per day:

>01.1 Greatness Redefined (Matt 20:25–28; Mark 10:42–45)
>
>* Focus: Shift from top-down control to bottom-up service.
>* Try today: Choose one hidden act that reduces someone else's burden.
>
>01.2 Authority vs. Title (Matt 23:8–12)
>
>* Focus: Untangle titles and true authority.
>* Try tomorrow: Drop one self-promoting behavior you might normally do; elevate someone else's contribution instead.
>
>01.3 Serving (Luke 22:25–27)
>
>* Focus: Seeking to be of service rather than being served.
>* Try next: Take the "lower" role in one interaction you'd usually reserve for a junior; e.g. do a task you would normally delegate, ask to help someone who usually serves you in some way.

Days 4–6: Repeat a practice daily; note what shifts in you mind, perspective, and your context.

Day 7: Review your notes; name one assumption about power that changed. If you go to a church service, notice how these ideas might show up there or change how you hear and understand it. Try to carry this new understanding into your week ahead.
:::
