## Received Teaching

::: aside
"Leaders must exercise authority to maintain order."[^0] 

It's a culturally upheld tradition that an authority figure; a person with a title; must exercise that authority frequently. The law enforcement officer who believes they must maintain control of people around them, or the judge who tries to make examples. It's said that the person "commands respect"; though in reality it could be just as well considered *demanding* respect. Power is considered only if it is asserted.

In any society, order requires some form of authority to enforce rules, resolve conflicts, and protect the community. Without authority, chaos could dominate. Philosophers like Rousseau and Locke argued that governance relies on a collective agreement to establish rules, implying that authority is necessary for societal cohesion. Essentially; the argument for the strong-man governance comes down to a sense of chaos and fear of that chaos, rather than some misguided semi-worship situation.
:::

## Jesus' Public Words

::: aside
"Ye know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. Not so shall it be among you: but whosoever would become great among you shall be your minister; and whosoever would be first among you shall be your servant: even as the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many."[^1]
:::
::: {.right}

## Reflection

1. Which words of power appear in your week? Where do you "lord it over" someone?
2. Actively imagine the experience of the least powerful person in your world this week.
3. Act: Choose one **hidden** service that reduces someone else’s burden.

## Practice

Each day, do one unnoticed or uncredited service for a lower-status or lower-powered colleague or neighbor; do not announce it.
:::
