## Received Teaching

::: aside
"Help your own first; outsiders aren’t your responsibility."[^10] Throughout history, people have argued that their own family should come before others. That their own countrymen, religion, or whatever other group be served before others. The idea that one cannot help others without helping themself is common; but also the idea of the person who can help everyone but themself is just as common. It is said that you should love your neighbor, and that is what people try to do. Defining neighbor is the tricky bit.
:::

## Jesus’ Public Words

::: aside
Jesus said, "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho; and he fell among robbers, who both stripped him and beat him, and departed, leaving him half dead. By chance, a certain priest was going down that way: and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side. And in like manner a Levite also, when he came to the place, and saw him, passed by on the other side. But a certain Samaritan, as he journeyed, came where he was: and when he saw him, he was moved with compassion, and came to him. He bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine; and he set him on his own beast, and brought him to an inn, and took care of him. And on the morrow he took out two shillings, and gave them to the host, and said, Take care of him; and whatsoever thou spendest more, I, when I come back again, will repay thee."[^11]
:::
::: {.right}

## Reflection

1. Observe: Who functions as your “Samaritan/outsider” today?
2. Empathize: What mercy would you want if you were in their place?
3. Act: Share time, money, or advocacy with one out‑group person.

## Practice

Do one meaningful act of mercy toward an outsider this week. Forgive a debt or favor, for instance.
:::
